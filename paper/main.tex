\title{Hidden Timers Break Long Rollouts of Pixel Diffusion World Models:\\
A Pre-registered Study on Ms.~Pac-Man}
\author{Sudish Mulakala}
\date{Draft, \today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We train an action-conditioned pixel diffusion world model of Ms.~Pac-Man in the style of DIAMOND~\cite{alonso2024diamond}
and ask how far one can play inside it before the world stops making sense.
The dominant long-horizon failure is a \emph{hidden timer}: the game holds ghosts in their pen for a fixed time (up to
91 steps) with no on-screen countdown. Models whose context sees only the last 4 or 8 frames park the ghosts
indefinitely: in 450-step rollouts on held-out episodes, 53--77\% of rollouts keep the pen occupied for 200+ steps,
against 0\% in the real game.
A context of the same frame count with a longer \emph{reach}, 4 recent frames plus 6 frames strided 16 steps apart,
removes the parking (0/30; paired difference to 4 frames $-0.57$, 95\% CI $[-0.77,-0.37]$ over episodes).
We state this as a rule: a model can time a hidden event of period $N$ only as precisely as its context offsets resolve
$-N$. We test the rule against an ideal observer on a synthetic game \todo{verdict}, and attempt a second Atari game
(the 2600 Pac-Man), whose pre-registered pilot stopped the study before the full runs.
We also report three negative or null results: DIAMOND's released Ms.~Pac-Man model, run in our harness, loses its
ghosts rather than parking them; a Genie-style latent action model~\cite{bruce2024genie} trained on our data collapsed
to one code; and several earlier claims (``10$\times$ data helps everything'') do not survive episode-level confidence
intervals.
All experiments were pre-registered before their results existed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Diffusion world models generate convincing game frames from a short context of past frames and actions
\cite{alonso2024diamond,valevski2024gamengen}. Their short context makes them vulnerable to state that the screen does
not show. Many games run clocks with no on-screen countdown: in Ms.~Pac-Man, ghosts wait in their pen for a fixed time
after a death, and a power pellet's effect lasts 124--134 steps. A model that sees only its last few frames cannot know
how long a ghost has been penned.

We make three contributions.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A documented failure and a fix.} With a 4- or 8-frame context, our 19M-parameter diffusion model parks
ghosts in the pen indefinitely; a strided context with the same number of frames but a 97-step reach removes the
parking without hurting other metrics (Sec.~\ref{sec:context}).
\item \textbf{A testable rule with an ideal-observer prediction.} The model's timing should match that of an observer
limited to the model's context offsets (Sec.~\ref{sec:rule}).
\item \textbf{A pre-registered, statistically honest evaluation.} The criteria were committed before each result
existed, CIs are bootstrapped over held-out episodes, and failures are reported (Secs.~\ref{sec:diamond},
\ref{sec:lam}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
\paragraph{Data.} \texttt{ALE/MsPacman-v5}~\cite{bellemare2013ale}, 4-frame skip with max-pooling over all 4 frames,
because Ms.~Pac-Man draws one ghost per frame and 2-frame pooling (the DQN default~\cite{mnih2015dqn}) shows all four
ghosts in only 29--47\% of observations. Maze crop $172\times160$, area-resized to $64\times64$. A PPO agent with
$\varepsilon$-random and sticky-random exploration recorded 2.2M frames (3,879 episodes). The validation split is frozen
by checksum: 388 episodes.

\paragraph{Model.} An EDM~\cite{karras2022edm} denoising UNet (18.8M params; widths 64/96/192/192, attention at 16 and
8 px). The noisy next frame is concatenated with $K$ context frames, and the actions enter through adaptive group
normalisation. It is trained with GameNGen-style context-noise augmentation~\cite{valevski2024gamengen} and sampled
with 3 Euler steps. Training: 100k steps at learning rate $10^{-4}$, followed by a 15k-step anneal at $10^{-5}$.

\paragraph{Context layouts.} The single variable of the ablation is the set of context offsets: \textbf{ctx4}
($-4..-1$), \textbf{ctx8} ($-8..-1$), and \textbf{ctx6s16} ($-97,-81,\dots,-17,-4..-1$: 10 frames, reach 97).

\paragraph{Evaluation.} Rollouts of 450 steps from step 100 of the 10 longest held-out episodes, 3 sampler seeds each,
replaying the recorded actions. Pixel detectors, validated on real frames, measure:
\begin{itemize}
\item Pac-Man position error;
\item wall IoU and pellet IoU;
\item ghost count, gated by RAM;
\item responsiveness: whether the model's Pac-Man turns where the recorded action turns him;
\item pen occupancy: any non-background pixels in the pen box.
\end{itemize}
CIs are 95\% bootstrap intervals over the 10 episodes (10,000 resamples). Each resampled episode keeps all three of its
seeds.

\paragraph{What holds.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Strided context removes the parking: the parked fraction falls by $-0.567$ $[-0.767,-0.367]$ against ctx4, and
by $-0.833$ $[-0.933,-0.733]$ in 900-step rollouts.
\item It also lowers Pac-Man error at 450 steps: $-5.3$ px $[-11.6,-0.5]$.
\item The LR anneal improves responsiveness: $+0.044$ $[+0.009,+0.080]$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What does not hold.}
\begin{itemize}
\item ``Eight frames park more than four'' is a trend only: $+0.20$ $[-0.10,+0.47]$.
\item Ten times more data does not change parking ($+0.03$ $[-0.20,+0.27]$) or Pac-Man error ($-0.5$ px
$[-2.7,+1.3]$); only responsiveness improves ($+0.071$ $[+0.014,+0.129]$).
\item No difference in ghost count between our models is significant.
\item A $128\times128$ version is sharper per step but drifts faster: Pac-Man error $+8.2$ px $[+1.9,+13.9]$ at
64px-equivalent.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The second timer is not solved.} The frightened timer (124--134 steps) exceeds the 97-step reach.
\begin{itemize}
\item No model ends frightened phases on time.
\item A 145-step-reach model (13 frames) ended them too early, and weakened the pen timer that 10 frames had fixed.
\item Teacher-forced analysis shows every model copies the phase end one step after it appears in a real frame, instead
of predicting it. The phase end is a one-frame event about once per episode, which the denoising loss barely rewards.
\end{itemize}
Reach therefore appears \emph{necessary but not sufficient}.

\section{The hidden-timer rule}
\label{sec:rule}
\paragraph{Rule.} An autoregressive frame model sees only its context. It can time a hidden-timer event of period $N$
only as precisely as its context offsets resolve $-N$:
\begin{itemize}
\item exactly, if $-N$ is an offset;
\item spread over the gap, if $-N$ falls between two offsets;
\item not at all beyond the reach $R$. The best possible behaviour is then a geometric wait with hazard $1/(N-R)$, and a
sampling model may do worse by parking.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Ideal observer.} For each layout we estimate, from training data, the hazard
$P(\text{event next}\mid\text{state at the layout's offsets})$, and roll this observer out from the same test starts
as the models. It is the best that \emph{any} model with those offsets can do, and its curve is the rule's prediction.
It is computed and committed before any model trains.

\paragraph{Synthetic game.}
\begin{itemize}
\item $16\times16$ frames. A ghost is penned for exactly $N$ frames; nothing on screen changes with elapsed time.
\item 23 cells cross three layouts with $N\in[3,200]$: C4 and C10 are consecutive with reach 4 and 10; S10 is ctx6s16's
layout, reach 97.
\item Small versions of our model, 15k steps each.
\item Pre-registered criteria: in reach, the model must come close to the observer; beyond reach, it must not beat an
observer blind to the capture.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{A real Atari game.}
\begin{itemize}
\item The 2600 Pac-Man holds a ghost in its house for exactly 35 or 36 steps after every death. The interval starts at
a visible event, has no on-screen countdown, and is detectable at 64px in 622 of 622 held-out stays.
\item The observer predicts a large contrast: S10 is on time in 0.889 of starts, C10 in 0.101.
\item The pre-registered pilot (S10 after 20k of 115k steps) had to release the ghost in at least 50\% of starts. It
released in 0.486 $[0.43, 0.54]$ and parked the rest, so the study stopped before training the full models.
\item This is a learnability failure at 20k steps. It is not a test of the rule.
\end{itemize}

\section{External baseline: DIAMOND}
\label{sec:diamond}
\paragraph{What we ran.} DIAMOND released its trained Atari-100k Ms.~Pac-Man world model (4-frame context). We ran it
without retraining: we re-simulated our held-out episodes in its preprocessing, which includes the score area and
2-frame pooling, and rebuilt our detectors for its geometry. All validity checks passed.

\paragraph{The registered prediction failed.} We had predicted that it would park ghosts in at least 10 of 30
rollouts. It parked in 0/30, because its ghosts disappear: its ghost count at 450 steps is 0.077 $[0.054,0.097]$ of its
own ground truth, against 0.41 for our 4-frame Model~1. This is neither support for the rule nor evidence against it.

\paragraph{Other metrics.} DIAMOND keeps the walls perfectly (wall IoU / ceiling 1.000) and is worse on Pac-Man error
(25.3 vs 14.6 px for our served model) and responsiveness (0.18 vs 0.43). DIAMOND was built for 15-step imagination
and trained on about $1/20$ of our data, so this comparison measures long-horizon coherence, not the merit of its design.

\section{Learned controls: a negative result}
\label{sec:lam}
\paragraph{Setup.} We trained a Genie-style latent action model~\cite{bruce2024genie} on frames only: 8 codes, a VQ
bottleneck~\cite{vandenoord2017vqvae} and a decoder with the 97-step context, under a label firewall tested by double
runs with scrambled labels. One arm was generic and one weighted the loss around Pac-Man with a pixel detector.

\paragraph{Result.} Both arms collapsed to one or two codes; code gain was exactly 1.000, so the decoder ignored the
code. The pre-registered stop fired before any label was read.

\paragraph{Diagnosis.} The decoder predicts the next frame well from context alone, in part because the recording
agent's moves are predictable from the screen. \todo{LAM v2 (4-frame decoder, entropy-regularised VQ, non-zero code
conditioning), if the owner approves and runs it.}

\section{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
\item One game for the main study, 64px frames, one seed per training configuration, and 10 evaluation episodes,
hence the wide CIs.
\item The synthetic test isolates timing but has no action conditioning.
\item The real-game test of the rule stopped at its pilot.
\item The frightened timer remains unsolved.
\item Responsiveness on replayed trajectories partly measures how predictable the agent is, not controllability.
\item The pen-occupancy measure is not resolution-invariant; at 128px its own ground truth differs.
\end{itemize}
